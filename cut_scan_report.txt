HOW TO READ THIS / METHOD + CAVEATS  (important -- the numbers need context)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Overlay videos are in ./diagnostics/<CLIP>_courtmap_overlay.mp4. Each draws a
  tracking PROBE anchored on the window's first frame -- a red court-region box,
  a cyan center-reference line, and a yellow dot grid -- then rides it with the
  tracker. The diagnostic question is simply: does the probe stay GLUED to the
  same floor spots as the camera moves? If it slides off the painted lines, the
  map is drifting and any team stat built on it is silently wrong.
  (It is NOT a calibrated court map -- per-clip calibration was out of scope.)

* "Hard cut" detection = independent frame-to-frame appearance-difference spikes
  (sudden whole-frame change => feed cut to another angle). Reported by
  frame/timestamp.

* "Lost lock" = the tracker's OWN internal signal (it could not estimate camera
  motion that frame and assumed the camera held still).

* CAVEAT 1 -- WINDOWS, NOT WHOLE CLIPS, for the Tactical files. They are FULL
  GAMES (83-100 min; 148,800-179,859 frames). An exhaustive per-frame overlay of
  a 100-min clip is hours of compute and multi-GB video, and the tracker must see
  consecutive frames so it can't be subsampled. So each Tactical clip was scanned
  on a representative 90-second window starting at 03:00. HARD (91s) was scanned
  in FULL. Whole-game passes are deferred (see TODO-A).

* CAVEAT 2 -- NO PLAYER MASKING in this diagnostic (it ran without YOLO, for
  speed). The real pipeline masks players out before tracking, which should
  REDUCE drift somewhat. So Tactical drift in production may be a bit better than
  shown here -- but the drift behavior itself is real.

* CAVEAT 3 -- drift is assessed VISUALLY from the overlays (no calibrated ground
  truth per clip). Treat drift magnitudes as approximate.
